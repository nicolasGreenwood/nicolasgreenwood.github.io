#### A note on this poem:

*It is not the first time the shadow of inevitability—rather unintentionally, I admit—has crept into my poetry. It is such a poetic idea, you know, to be aware of one’s own nonsense, to sing of the unpreventable. This is not always a negative thing. In this poem, however, it is.*

*This is, unapologetically, a composition on oppression. Not just any oppression, but the kind where the line separating the oppressor and the oppressed blurs. Such is the worst kind of tormentor: the kind who torments by causing the victim to torment themselves. Their return, after so many visits, comes as no surprise to the narrator.*

---

- Truly, it comes as no surprise
- to see you again with your
- enflamed face and bloodied eyes
- and your harsh, unending, hellish cries.
{.poetry}
- I say, I’ve known this face before
- from beneath sleepless eyelids sore
- from tears and terror-stricken nights
- and clouded skies and blinding lights
- and subtle knocking at my door.
{.poetry}
- Truly, it comes as no surprise
- to hear you again, as within
- my own heart sinks and organs rise
- to fill my mouth with blood and lies
- I swallow before your very eyes.
{.poetry}
- Like an old friend you stop by
- of bygone days to reflect and query why
- my brow is fresh and eyelids dry
- my hands are clean and dreams are sweet
- and how dare I hold my head so high!
{.poetry}
- Like a tender father you bend the knee
- stroke my cheek and ask of me
- how I could’ve strayed from the
- only way you’ve taught to me
- and ventured past the gate to see
- from beyond the hills the sunlight rise
- and dry this pair of tear-stained eyes
- —no, indeed, your thought of me
- —truly, it comes as no surprise.
