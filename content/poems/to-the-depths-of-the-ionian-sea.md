*In memoriam, Vladislav Petković Dis*

*(1880-1917)*

---

- We sink, for the shore's to far to see
- We fall from a dark and ominous sky
- To the depths of the Ionian Sea.
{.poetry}
- From the deep we pulled up our anchors three
- With bleeding hands we raised our colors high
- We sink, for the shore's too far to see.
{.poetry}
- We dream of nothing but eternity
- We descend to a place where great men lie
- To the depths of the Ionian Sea.
{.poetry}
- Lo, our hearts yet beat, our eyes yet see!
- Oh hollow deep! Drown not our cry!
- We sink, for the shore's too far to see.
{.poetry}
- O'er life Fate has chosen serenity
- To eternal rest like birds we fly
- To the depths of the Ionian Sea.
{.poetry}
- To our tombs ordained by Deity
- Below the waves' unceasing lullaby
- We sink, for the shore's too far to see
- To the depths of the Ionian Sea.

Vladislav Petković Dis was one of the greatest Serbian impressionist poets. During my time in Čačak, Serbia, I had the privilege to volunteer for some time in the district library, which is named after him. Dis’s birthplace, Zablaće, is a small village only a few kilometers outside the city of Čačak. As my days of volunteering drew closer to an end at his library, writing a poem in his commemoration was all but inevitable.

Petković Dis, as you may have noticed reading his life dates above, did not live a very long life. With a weak constitution that followed him to his final day, Dis’s life is one marked with moments of great joy but also great tragedy. On May 30th, 1917, at the age of 37, while returning from France to Corfu to rejoin his exiled nation on the Salonica front of WWI, a torpedo from an Austro-Hungarian U-boat sunk the passenger steamship carrying Dis, sinking the poet and many on board to the depths of the Ionian Sea.
