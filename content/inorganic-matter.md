## Author's note

The title of this collection came to me suddenly one day, without
warning, as I tried to think of a random set of words to use as filler
text in a design project I was working on. From the moment I said it
to myself, I couldn’t get it out of my head: Inorganic Matter. It
sounded bold, unconventional—almost avant-garde. It felt like it
was describing something new; some fresh, unusual perspective
just waiting to take shape in a poem.

Months later—after many of the poems in this collection were
already written—I finally understood why this cold, clinical phrase
refused to leave my thoughts. It perfectly expressed the chilling
lifelessness hiding behind everything I saw and touched. All at
once, I realized that all matter was once inorganic, and yet it has
somehow become organic—able to live, observe, think, and even
love.

This realization fascinated me enough to title a collection after it.
Not every poem in this book is explicitly about this transformation.
But, if you take a closer look, perhaps that isn’t entirely true.
Perhaps every poem ever written is, in some way, about a
conscious experience of some sort. And perhaps, after reading
that, you’ll see why I chose a title broad enough to hold even the
most unrelated pieces together.

Allow me a short piece of advice for reading this work. Poetry
collections rarely read like novels, and this is no exception. Read a
poem, and close the book. There is no need to fully understand it.
Sometimes, all you need is a single line that feels insightful—or at
least, relatable.

Happy reading,

### *Proleće* (Spring)

#### Part 1: *Proleće* from behind a window

- It came one morning
- When I was asleep it came
- I opened my eyes to a
- flood of sunlight
- crystal sky
- kissing breeze.
{.poetry}
- Lo, darkness had faded away
- Faded away
- Given way to the power of light.
- Curtains slipped through my fingers
- To the window was my forehead pressed
- I closed my eyes
- And let tears stain the glass
- — the glass was cold
- The sun was warm
- The sun was hot.
- It was golden! — it washed my face in gold
- It was light
- I could feel light
- Power
- The power of life.
{.poetry}

#### Part 2: *Proleće* from beneath trees

- She skipped through the tall grass
- The hem of her white linen brushing over the blades
- I followed.
{.poetry}
- The world flew past
- by me
- around me
- through me
- into me.
- I heard laughter
- Everything was blurry
- was spinning.
{.poetry}
- I brushed my hands over those blades
- Like the hem of her white linen
- My steps took me onward
- I was running
- I was not running
- I was dancing
- I was not dancing
- I was flying.
{.poetry}
- The trees rustled above
- Rays of sunlight flickered through them
- A million flashes
- Through a million leaves
- — I smiled
- As my fingers brushed the rough bark of a trunk.
- There was a scent in the air
- Of something fresh
- Something from long ago
- That I had once forgotten
- That I no longer forget
- Nor remember.
{.poetry}
- We fall to the grass
- watch clouds
- finger buds
- pick dandelions
- feel our heartbeats.
{.poetry}
- Laughter again
- My eyes are closed
- There are hands on my shoulders
- Breath in my ear.
{.poetry}

#### Part 3: *Proleće* from along sidewalks

- One by one they slide past
- — they’re faces, human faces
- they smile
- they blink
- they turn away.
{.poetry}
- ‘Tis a cold wind blowing through these streets
- Winding through alleyways
- — an airy serpent —
- It slithers around buildings
- Buildings colored pastel
- With marble busts agape
- With marble figures adance.
{.poetry}
- Sunlight glitters on concrete
- I run my fingers over graffiti stains
- I stop and ponder scene
- after scene
- after scene.
- There is a wrinkled face with the brightest eyes
- There are raven locks whipped by the wind
- Webs of dead branches tipped with life
- Shoes stepping on crosswalks
- Ashes floating in the breeze.
{.poetry}
- I walk on
- The cries of children in my ears
- Against the background of
- That gentle cacophony of urban traffic
- Knocking over
- A basket of
- apricots, their juice a sweet syrup
- Glinting in the afternoon sun.
{.poetry}

#### Part 4: *Proleće* from beneath stars

- The starry host
- They’re a great choir
- One great choir
- Of silence.
- I could hear their singing
- As we lay on the cool grass
- Our arms outstretched
- Embracing the sky.
{.poetry}
- All over the earth
- There are whispers — thousands of whispers
- The cricket’s song
- The katydid’s chant
- The bird’s lullaby
- The mother’s goodnight.
- I heard them all that night
- All at once.
{.poetry}
- The world is alive
- The world is born again
- The world is hushed
- in anticipation.
- For now it is dark
- And night is the time to dream
- Of what will take place on the following day:
- Buds will unfurl
- Wings will poise and take flight
- Water will dance and roar before the sun
- A dragonfly’s wings will reflect a rainbow
- — the story of spring will be retold
- As it has
- For a time longer than our lives.
{.poetry}
- Like a hand pressed against a pane of glass
- So I reached for the void
- Stretched above us
- Spinning ever so slowly
- Dancing the steady waltz of time
- Too slow to perceive
- Yet too swift to risk
- Letting your eyes close
- Before they fade away.
- I reached for it
- And somehow
- On that strange, fragrant night
- My feeble hand
- Though many a light year away from their burning crowns
- I knew had touched
- Their eternal glow
- If for only one fleeting instant.
{.poetry}
- May the immortal touch the mortal!
- May the light touch the dark!
- May life touch death!
- May sunlight touch the frost and
- May spring touch the winter.
{.poetry}

### To the Butterfly

- Sunlight glowed through the orange translucence
- Of her delicate wings patterned with black;
- Vibrance bursted in green luminescence
- Through the leaves above waving forth and back.
{.poetry}
- In delighted frenzy she fluttered on
- Through a forest of light, beauty untold;
- The beads of dew lit by the rays of dawn
- Flew past beneath her in flashes of gold.
{.poetry}
- Her life’s a dream but a single day long
- From dawn to dusk lasts her transient flight
- ‘Tis a life too short to understand wrong
- For a soul too pure to ever see night.
{.poetry}
- In blissful ignorance her day flies past
- With rapturous delight at every sight;
- Every flower a beauty unsurpassed
- Each moment closer to eternal night.
{.poetry}
- March, butterflies, to the grand drum of Time —
- Flutter and dance while your hearts still thunder;
- Plunge in that intoxication divine
- Let euphoria rend you asunder.
{.poetry}
- Pause not for a moment in your pursuit
- Waste not an instant of your life in thought!
- Life’s a withering tree so reap its fruit
- Snatch the sweetest clusters before they rot.
{.poetry}
- Flutter and dance into the jaws of death
- Yes, dance as a symbol of defiance;
- In beauty’s madness breathe your final breath
- Embrace death in defiant compliance.
{.poetry}
- For in the futility of it all
- Resides a great, unbearable beauty;
- Keep your eyes fixed on the light as you fall —
- Dance, oh butterfly, as is your duty!
{.poetry}

### Lily of the Valley
- I run under trees through morning mist
- when all is blue and the branches of trees
- are shadows resembling fractals of ink.
{.poetry}
- Dew-tipped grasses rest their heads on me
- and cobwebs hang in dark, sticky places
- beneath arching branches bent over with leaves.
{.poetry}
- I breathe in and fly down a curved gravel road
- I make my way through air thick and wet
- with the green, sticky substance from which life is made.
{.poetry}
- I descend into darkness, where tree trunks and leaves
- are the sun and the sky, and the dandelion seed
- is alive and flies and perches on my shoulder.
{.poetry}
- The end of the trail is a sloping descent
- into a valley of softness, and the feeling of dew
- sprouting freshly on my cheek is a single tear wept
- by the face of another, a face framed by flowers.
{.poetry}
- In a light I am bathed, a gentle white light
- from a tender stem glowing, and a blossoming sun
- bent sadly downwards, cupped with round petals
- rains soft, silken rays illuminating the ground.
{.poetry}

### Blades of Grass

{.poetry}

{.poetry}

{.poetry}

{.poetry}

- it does not scream

{.poetry}

{.poetry}

- It does not know.
- It does not understand.
- But tears—
- it wipes away.
{.poetry}
- No time has passed.
- The moment yet lives.
{.poetry}
- On blades of grass I lie;
- they bend in the wind.

### I Wrote This Beneath Stars

- A bottle of ink spilled onto a sheet of paper
- My tears fell too
- It dried into a poem.
{.poetry}
- Did you know that even stars die?
- I whisper to them, you are eternal!

- is existence beyond when I cease to exist.
{.poetry}

- So that you could become eternal
- Like the stars.

### A Mist Made of Stars

- I am moving swiftly, and stand still
- engulfed in endlessness and confined
- to the distance my outstretched arms can reach;
- in darkness I blindly grope
- for light, and for its fiber and glow,
- its warmth upon my cheek,
- to see tears sparkle like glass beads
- on my hands and in dreams
- to bathe in its blueness once again;
- eternity, and the knot of dread
- grips my stomach as vastness
- envelopes my body and enters
- the heart beneath my ribs; I
- look and drift when light, in the
- shape of a single
- brushstroke and as dim as a
- *muscae volitantes* appears and
- grows and
- becomes a cloud, a fine mist
- made from light, from flames, and from stars;
- now this glow is cool and fresh and
- mysterious, like a spirit, and clearing
- my vision, washing it from darkness; coldness
- it is, and lifeless—solitary, and as merciful
- as that cabin that shelters
- the winter traveller; there are webs
- of light moving past, clusters of light, almost organic
- in shape and form, and dust
- glows like clouds in a photograph
- of undeveloped film; suddenly
- this mist becomes discernible and
- particles emerge from regularity; they are
- specks of light with jagged edges—an
- infinity-pointed star; they blur into eye water and
- burn my vision when my eyelids close;
- they form streaks,
- and streaks form circles and spirals
- and, like comets, their trail burns
- out slowly behind them with decreasing intensity;
- I look,
- I close my eyes, and I cannot escape
- their fire; I cannot turn away
- from these visions and
- these flashes no matter
- how quickly I turn my head; my jaw clenches and
- my tongue is bitter inside my mouth; I refuse to breathe
- until this light fades away, until these flames
- cease to lick at my cornea, when
- suddenly blackness, inky and merciful
- coats my eyes, and in oblivion
- I find rest; this blackness is so perfectly black
- that it is almost brown; solid, and so vast and empty
- that when I reach out, there is nothing to touch;
- I feel small—
- small and warm again; my head does not hurt
- nor does light burn me; I do not feel
- my heart beneath my ribs nor does
- blue coldness tell me I am dreaming and there is
- no bitterness and
- no sweetness either but there is only
- everything and
- nothing.
{.poetry}

### Gravity

- Crimson red were the leaves and
- wet were the lands and streets. Faulty
- were the eyes that beheld your beauty
- and empty were the arms that embraced you.
- And even though wind in my ear whispers and
- silver clouds above do quiver in my teardrops,
- your touch remained forever just beyond my reach.
{.poetry}
- I confess, nights did pass
- with sleeplessness; I burned candles, and flames
- did blind my eyes. Creases in fabric became
- my meditation, and light—the kind not seen, but felt;
- white and pure,
- Impossible and unbearable—
- did pierce my closed, dreaming eyes. And yet,
- your face, so unforgettable,
- was impossible to remember.
{.poetry}
- I confess, into a field I went
- into a meadow I wandered and
- into a sky I looked.
- I confess, my head I threw back
- and of flying I dreamed.
{.poetry}
- *Among clouds and birds I wished to be*
- *the earth below I longed to see*
- *further and further away from me—*
- *but it was not to be.*
{.poetry}
- And yet though all this the clock did tick;
- the sun did move and its zenith cross
- and into the sky I did not ascend
- nor could your face remember.
{.poetry}
- Cold air I did breathe and water colder
- did I taste; clouds and dust
- and leaves and ceilings
- did hide your face from view. Birds
- I watched ascend; their eyes I saw
- moist and pearly from venturing so near
- the sun and on mountains tall
- I gazed and wept, whose peaks, so close to heaven,
- were swept with wind, and where ice
- did crack and crumble; in the gusts
- I hoped for salvation; of riding their wings I dreamed;
- to be carried in their arms I longed.
{.poetry}
- Gravity it was to blame; I could feel him in each
- and every step, and when I slept, his weight
- did slowly bend and distort my limbs; his pressure
- did squeeze and crack my temples and lower
- my line of sight to the ground. The sky,
- for which I reached and opened my chest,
- he pulled me from and to my ankles
- clung; from up there, in the endless blue
- he held me, and from my sight of you.
{.poetry}
- *Among clouds and birds I wished to be*
- *the earth below I longed to see*
- *further and further away from me—*
- *but it was not to be.*
{.poetry}

### Asking for Love

- He is afraid
- to ask for love.
- Because
- he might be embarrassed
- or she might think him strange.
- Because
- certainly so many have asked her before
- and over time she has come
- to dread and despise it.
- Because
- it embarrasses her,
- and she does not feel pleasant.
- Because
- she might have to say no, and that is not
- what the asker or anyone likes to hear.
- One day he asks her, because he simply can’t bear
- to see her and watch her and not find out what
- could possibly happen
- if he did such a thing.
- He asks for something
- that she will not give.
- She tells him no,
- but he persists.
- She becomes angry and
- he only laughs.
- She becomes more angry, and
- he takes offense.
- He becomes angry too, and
- she takes offense.
- They are both very angry, and
- they both take offense.
- They both storm off in a
- frightful rage.
- They both feel guilt
- a while later that day
- and a tinge of disbelief that
- such awful things
- could be uttered by their own and the other’s mouth.
- They both, for a day, and
- a week, and
- a month,
- avoid each other’s glances and
- stiffly ignore
- the very existence of
- the other one’s person.
- Until, one day, in the sunshine of May
- the face once ignored
- appears more beautiful than
- what was remembered,
- and the burning in
- their chest had now vanished.
- Words not said
- became words gently said and
- hands not held
- became hands gently held and
- lips not kissed
- became lips gently kissed and
- he was not afraid
- to ask for love.
{.poetry}

### Absence

- For some reason I do not know
- Your hand slipped away from my own
- It slipped away without a word
- In silence, I was left alone.
{.poetry}
- I asked the stars to tell me why
- From a life your presence had graced
- You faded away so softly
- To a city so distant placed;
{.poetry}
- They replied not a word at all
- I was left flying through their light
- On a field wet with starlit dew
- In a dream with no end in sight.
{.poetry}
- My tears fell into a river
- It carried them down to the sea
- There in that tomb of mankind’s tears
- To billow for eternity.
{.poetry}
- Then in the wind I heard your voice
- Not a word — alas — just a moan
- A sweet-breathed whisper on my face
- Though the night from a land unknown.
{.poetry}
- I felt your touch in the cold gusts
- Your presence borne upon its wings
- Filling my chest with shards of you
- A sculpture made of broken things.
{.poetry}
- I embraced that sweet empty place
- A spirit made of thoughts of you
- For one short night, before the time
- To vanish, once again, was due.
{.poetry}

### Three Thoughts While Riding a Bus

#### Thought 1

- The bar is round
- But it does not feel round in my hand.
{.poetry}
- The bar must be round
- But it feels like an elliptical cylinder.
{.poetry}
- This is a game of motion and momentum;
- There is a window, a bar and a shifting floor
- Which you must use to stand and not fall.
{.poetry}
- And through this window there is a sight
- From which you will not be able to tear your eyes
- For the world siding past through an aperture
- Is another from the one standing still, surrounding.
{.poetry}

#### Thought 2

- This face holds a specific position
- Like a mask, when the head moves, it follows, motionless.
{.poetry}
- This face glows a specific hue
- Like a mask, there are no eyes, but dark hollows.
{.poetry}
- Silently, it leans gently on glass
- Bathed in sunlight, beautiful and
- artificial, like an overblown, high contrast photograph.
{.poetry}
- It is not a face, but a sun—
- So it seems, as my eyes slowly close
- —it is a star, with rays of its own
- And warmth; fiery, life-giving, impossible to behold.
{.poetry}

#### Thought 3

- Swept over wet pavement, the wind
- blowing through the window ajar, is cool and fresh.
{.poetry}
- Cutting like ice over tear-moistened eyes
- Touching strands of hair, it caresses.
{.poetry}
- I know this wind; my planet
- Has made a revolution and a little more since I last felt
- Its tresses smother my respiration.
{.poetry}
- It is the very same air that rushed through my lungs
- When sweet weight fell onto me and cold water
- Dripped from my fingers and glittered in the sun.
{.poetry}
- I step out into this air, with the sun shining on me.
{.poetry}

### Smoking

### That Thing

When you met That Thing, it was love at first sight. There's
no better feeling than thinking about it, admiring it, loving
it. Why, the very dreams of life were That Thing! You
embraced it, it embraced you back, and you felt like one.
And then, something happens. It always does. It’s called
Time. It passes. It always does. You cannot focus and love
one thing all the time, for a long time. And then, one day,
you revisited That Thing, and it’s gone. It’s left. It’s not
there anymore, and the only thing that remained was its
shadow and maybe a lingering outline in the air.
{.poetry}
And then, one day, you meet That Thing. You meet it on
the road, unexpectedly, and you’re overjoyed. You say
hello but shake their hand instead of embracing them.
And then, you realize that their clothes have changed, and
they smell different. Their hairstyle has changed too. But
most importantly, they hardly recognize you. They’ve
forgotten you. And you laugh and remind them of the fun
things you’ve done together, and they laugh too, hollowly,
and with a tinge of confusion on their face.
{.poetry}
And then, you move on the road. You pick up your pace.
Somehow, the feeling of meeting them is worse than if
you’d never met them at all. How unexpectedly expected.
You are sick, and your spit is bitter in your mouth. You walk
and breathe, and the sky and the road spin around and
around you. You love this thing, and you loathe this thing.
You stumble on, step after step, and you want to sleep.
{.poetry}
Then you find a tree—a thick, beautiful, ancient tree. Its
bark is smooth and its leaves are lush and green. You
touch this tree, lean on this tree, sit by this tree. You love
this tree. Somehow, this tree consoles you. In its cool
shade, you’re sheltered from the dusty, sunburnt road and
bitter spit. And the rustling of its branches in the wind is
so spontaneous and oblivious to the world that you too
feel spontaneous and oblivious to the world.
You fall asleep. And you forget That Thing.
{.poetry}

## Part 2: *Notes*

---

### A Note on *Proleće (Spring)*

*Proleće* is the result of months of observing springtime here in
Serbia. When I moved to this country a few years ago and
experienced a Serbian spring for the first time, I was overwhelmed
with how lush, green, sunny, and dreamy it felt. The air had a kind ofunique floral scent I'd never inhaled before and the general
atmosphere was hopeful in a completely new and beautiful way.

Less than a kilometer from where I lived, there was a long path
winding beside a river through fields and patches of forest. It was
this place that inspired much of this poem. I would walk here
almost every day, taking in the powerful lushness of Serbian spring.

A few months after writing *Proleće*, I had the privilege of being able to present both an English and a translated Serbian version of the
last part of the poem, Proleće From Beneath Stars, to a live
audience during an event hosted by the City Library of Čačak,
where I also worked as a volunteer for several months.

---

### A Note on *To the Butterfly*

This poem was also inspired by my long riverside walks. The area
was rich with wildlife and I remember thinking as I watched
butterflies flutter across the path about their short, beautiful little
existences.

I was also inspired by Serbian author Desanka Maksimović's
brilliant work *Bajka o Kratkovečnoj*, a children's story following a
butterfly with a very short lifespan.

---

### A Note on *Lily of the Valley*

There is something inexplicably magical about walking in a forest
early in the morning, before the sun rises. The grass and flowers are
wet with dew and all of our familiar places somehow look
dramatically different. Flowers begin to open and light itself feels
strangely dim and precious.

---

### A Note on *Flowers*
One of the saddest things I have ever seen is a gravestone with old,
wilted flowers from months, if not years ago.

“So long as we are being remembered, we remain alive.”

- Carlos Ruiz Zafón, *The Shadow of the Wind*

---

### A Note on *Blades of Grass*

Grass is uniquely poetic. Like stars, it simply exists, whether we
notice it or not. This poem was partly inspired by Walt Whitman's
poem *Song of Myself, 6 [A child said, What is the grass?]*, part of his
collection *Leaves of Grass*, which is also widely considered one of
the greatest pieces of American poetry ever written.

---

### A Note on *I Wrote This Beneath Stars*

I did indeed physically write this poem beneath a starry sky, but the
true significance of this piece lies in the realization that eternity, as
illogical as it sounds, is very much a subjective concept. Eternity is
only as large as you can imagine it to be.

---

### A Note on *A Mist Made of Stars*

The title of the poem came to me suddenly, as I took a quick glance
up at the night sky before entering a doorway. The initial idea was
to convey the massive scale of galaxies and groups of stars by
using the term "mist" to describe their quantity. As I wrote, however,
the poem took more of a philosophical turn. It asks the question,
how safe are we in our ignorance? How much light are we ready to
take?

---

### A Note on *Sleepless*

It has always struck me as unbelievable and tragic that so many of
us, in the second quarter of the 21st century, still spend their nights
on benches. I have always wondered, what is it like to fall asleep
night after night under an open sky? The nights must feel
unbearably long and strangely peaceful.

---

### A Note on *Gravity*

Gravity, like circumstance, holds us down so constantly that we are
hardly aware of it. And yet, sometimes I suddenly feel it. I feel dizzy
and notice its pressure on my head and body ever pulling me
downwards. Limitation is one way to distinguish between dreams
and reality.

---

### A Note on *To the Eagle*

This poem is very much an extension of the previous. The words
came to me all at once as I watched a bird in flight so high above
me that it was nothing but a sliver barely visible in the blue.

### A Note on *Asking for Love*

This poem unexpectedly came to be as I was writing out the
structure of a poem. Somehow, I found the unusual, rigid style
peculiarly charming, and I decided to complete the poem in the
same style.

In this modern world of internet and fast-paced lifestyles,
loneliness and lack of human connection are things I hear people
complain about all the time. And they aren't wrong to do so.
Relationships of many kinds are essential for mental health and
well-being. Unfortunately, it is becoming increasingly harder to
make new connections when so many of us spend most of our free
time alone in front of a screen or doing other solitary activities.

This poem suggests a simple solution: just ask. Take the step—don't
be afraid! Approach that person you like and don't take whatever
they might say too personally. They too are a person just like you,
with just the same doubts and fears. Love them, respect them, and
let time do its work.

---

### A Note on *The Blue*

There is this euphoric, carefree feeling I sometimes get when I'm
walking outside somewhere under a clear blue sky. Somehow, my
vision feels clearer and everything seems more beautiful than I
remember it. This is a poem about that feeling.

---

### A Note on *Dreaming of You*

In my city there is a large, white bridge stretching over the Danube.
I remember gazing at it and feeling the warm, heady, and slightly
sickening sensation of missing someone on a bright, dusty August
day—very similar to the rush of vertigo that comes when we lean
over the railing of a bridge and gaze at the water below.

---

### A Note on *You Remind Me*

This is a piece on déjà vu—but only the delightful kind.

---

### A Note on *Faces*

This question has always intrigued me: do we meet people we love,
or do we love people we meet? Whatever the answer, I think both
realities are equally beautiful.

---

### A Note on *Absence*

This is a tribute to all those I’ve had to leave behind as well as a
reflection on the nature of memory and how memories, no matter
how beautiful or vivid, can never replace seeing those we love and
think of the most in real life.

---

### A Note on *I See Faces I Will Not See Again*

Strangers leave a deeper impression on me than I often admit.
Sometimes I can recall a completely random face I only ever saw
once for years afterwards. I've always wondered about the
explanation for this. This poem describes this experience, and not
without a tinge of melancholy.

---

### A Note on *Three Thoughts While Riding a Bus*

Riding buses was something I hadn't done very much prior to
moving to Europe, and to this day it remains a memorable
experience every time I do it.

Being in such close proximity to so many unknown people is
certainly one reason for that. As someone who observes others
perhaps a little too much, every trip to town on board a bus
invariably turns into a deep psychological and philosophical
reverie.

This poem doesn't try to do anything more than describe a few
glimpses of my thought process when I'm on board.

---

### A Note on *Streetlight*

Like many of my poems, Streetlight was inspired by one of my long
walks. I would walk through my city after sunset, when the streets
were largely empty and there was nothing but dim, silent light
glowing from streetlamps down on the sidewalk.

### A Note on *Smoking*

### A Note on *Vreme (Time)*

As the first poem I have ever written originally in Serbian, this piece
will always be special to me.

This poem is less about the swiftness of time and more about its
irreversibility. Not long before writing it, I had spent some time
learning about our best physical theories and understandings of
what time actually is. While that question remains largely
unanswered, one thing is certain: time moves linearly in a positive
direction, and it does not seem obvious that we will ever find a way
to reverse it.

---

### A Note on *Horizon*

This is a poem about insignificance and determination in spite of it.
We confidently plan and imagine our futures, but do we really
understand how much chance and circumstance influence our life’s
course? It is better to live and adapt than to plan and end up giving
up.

---

### A Note on *Another Day Ended*

This is a piece on that peculiar, slightly unsettling feeling that
comes when a day is ending and you have hardly noticed it slip by.
We dismiss it and assure ourselves that tomorrow will be better,
longer, and more memorable—until the next evening comes and we
find ourselves thinking the exact same thing for the following day.

Time progresses steadily at the same velocity it always has—it is
only our perception of it that changes. I wrote this poem to remind
myself of all the possibilities in a single day and that our entire lives,
in a sense, are only as long as the amount of time between now and
the sunset.

---

### A Note on *Dear Yellow,*

Yellow is a special color to me. Sometimes it strikes as the most
gorgeous, joyful color I've seen, while other times I can't bear to
look at it. I haven't quite make up my mind.

There is a specific yellow I particularly like, and that is this pale,
pastille yellow I see very often here in Europe on historic,
architectural buildings. For some reason, I see it everywhere I go.

---

### A Note on *Truly, It Comes As No Surprise*

It is not the first time the shadow of inevitability—rather
unintentionally, I admit—has crept into my poetry. It is such a poetic
idea, you know, to be aware of one’s own nonsense, to sing of the
unpreventable. This is not always a negative thing. In this poem,
however, it is.

This is, unapologetically, a composition on oppression. Not just any
oppression, but the kind where the line separating the oppressor
and the oppressed blurs. Such is the worst kind of tormentor: the
kind who torments by causing the victim to torment themselves.
Their return, after so many visits, comes as no surprise to the
narrator.

---

### A Note on *To Pain I Say*

This poem came to be when I asked myself, if pain were a person,
what would you tell them? Besides the discomfort, pain does a few
good things: it slows down the ever-speeding clock and clings
faithfully to you in its own, unpleasant way. There is something to
be grateful for in everything.

---

### A Note on *Walk*

I wrote this poem as a piece of advice to myself. There are moments
when even the immediate future is entirely uncertain and there is
no right or wrong solution. The only way forward it to let time pass
and place one foot after another until circumstances change on
their own.

---

### A Note on *He Watches Me*

I vividly recall sitting alone at a desk in a public library, writing
something, when I suddenly felt a pair of eyes turned at me. I
looked up and noticed a portrait of Ivo Andrić (a famous Yugoslav
author and Nobel Prize laureate) hanging on the wall, his serious
face watching me quietly. For a moment, it seemed like he was
really there, observing me, reminding me by his presence alone to
write and only write the best of myself. I left that day feeling his
gaze follow me beyond the doorways of the library.

---

### A Note on *To Styx*

It can be deceptively easy to lose hope and a generally positive
view of the future. To truly empathize with someone suffering from
depression, you have to understand their perspective and way of
thinking. One common misconception is that depressed individuals
are afraid of taking their own life. More often than not, the exact
opposite is the case—in their perceived situation, thoughts of self
destruction are seen as a way out, and thus soothing and even
pleasant.

There was a bridge over a river not far from my home I would
sometimes stand on and watch the water below me. It was eerily
consoling.

---

### A Note on *I Will Read Myself a Poem*

Reading poetry is the sweetest form of escapism when the writing
is vivid and relatable. It can be done in any place, at any time of day
or night, and the experience will hardly vary.

Sometimes I read a poem to freshen my general perspective and
reset my thoughts. Other days I read poetry to escape from reality
and exist in that absurd, restrictionless world of words.

---

### A Note on *That Thing*

There is something so confusing and disorienting about having and
then suddenly losing interest in something. This is a poem on
transience—the somewhat nihilistic observation that no passion or
love lasts forever and that sometimes, they can end sooner and
more suddenly than we expect.

---

### A Note on *Bipolar*

This poem is less about psychological disorder than it is about how
suddenly and strangely our thoughts can oscillate between light
and gloom.

---

### A Note on *Into the Tomb We Go*
I wrote this poem for the 10th anniversary of my father’s passing.
It’s usually very difficult to write poetry on demand, and this time
was no exception. Even though I veered in a more philosophical
direction with this piece than I had originally intended, that’s by no
means an inaccurate description of my experience with grief.
Sometimes I find myself thinking about it, observing it from a
distance—like loss didn’t happen to me. To me, grief takes the
shape of a dull, mild ache suddenly replaced by the realization of
my own mortality and the ground beneath my feet slowly inching
closer to my vision.

---

### A Note on *To the Depths of the Ionian Sea*

Vladislav Petković Dis was one of the greatest Serbian
impressionist poets. During my time in Čačak, Serbia, I had the
privilege to volunteer for some time in the district library, which is
named after him. Dis’s birthplace, Zablaće, is a small village only a
few kilometers outside the city of Čačak. As my days of
volunteering drew closer to an end at his library, writing a poem in
his commemoration was all but inevitable.

Petković Dis, as you may have noticed reading his life dates above
the poem, did not live a very long life. With a weak constitution that
followed him to his final day, Dis’s life is one marked with moments
of great joy but also great tragedy. On May 30th, 1917, at the age of
37, while returning from France to Corfu to rejoin his exiled nation
on the Salonica front of WWI, a torpedo from an Austro-Hungarian
U-boat sunk the passenger steamship carrying Dis, sinking the
poet and many on board to the depths of the Ionian Sea.

---

### A Note on *Dear Pacific,*

Massive bodies of water, and especially oceans, fascinate me. I’ve
been fortunate enough to stand on the shore of the Pacific on the
American western coast multiple times in my life, and every time it
has never failed to convince me that the human mind cannot and
86
will never be able to fully comprehend the vastness of our oceans.
We stand there, on the shore, watching the horizon, and all we can
see of this incomprehensibly gigantic body of water is a meagre 5
kilometers out, at most—not even the tiniest, visible distance when
we look at a map of the world or a globe. And all we see is the
surface. Below is yet another world, with its own mountains and
valleys, stories and civilizations. With its own view of the sun, moon
and stars. And with its own depths the size of continents that rays
of sunlight have never touched.

There is also something inexplicably special and awe-inspiring
about experiencing a storm on a beach. With all that open space in
front of you, the wind is incredibly powerful and that combined
with the roaring of the surf creates an absolutely overpowering
sensory experience.

Dear Pacific is an exploration of these images and emotions.

---

### A Note on *From Dust*

From Dust is a poem on human fragility. It has always fascinated
and terrified me in equal measure that all of our thoughts, emotions,
and all that we know live in such a small, sensitive lump of watery
tissue tucked away in our cranial cavity. From this perspective, it
feels almost miraculous that we are even capable of living,
observing, philosophying, understanding—loving. This poem, like
this entire collection, is a confession of love against that
background—an acknowledgement of our fragility and humble,
inorganic origin and a defiant declaration of love in spite of it.

---

### A Note on *Bread*

The feeling of standing in an Orthodox church or monastery is truly
unlike anything else I have ever experienced. Since relocating to
Europe, I've had many, many more opportunities to visit these
places—some of them old as the 13th century. One thing that really
left an impression on me was how the eyes of the saints depicted in
the frescoes lining the walls remain fixed on you as you walk past
them. Standing inside one of these places feels like what I imagine
the afterlife would be like: surrounded on all sides with with
endless, deep, Byzantine blue as shining faces watch me intently
with their anagogic gaze.

---

### A Note on *Hunger*
We read about hunger every day on the news, see pictures of it,
walk past it on the street—even feel tinges of it throughout the day,
but do we really notice it? Or do we simply turn our heads away
and think of something more pleasant until, one day, we find
ourselves laying on the ground, clinging to conciousness,
wondering if anyone is thinking of us?

I believe art has the power to spark empathy unlike any other
medium.

---

### A Note on *"Wecome, Little One."*

Children are born into this world with fresh minds and limitless
authenticity, but before they even know for themselves, the
process of shaping and restricting our identity and imagination has
already begun. This poem is not a protest againt rules and order,
but rather against the harmful aspects of societal structure and a
warning about the dangers of forcing pointless comformity.

---

### A Note on *"Greetings, sir."*

This is a poem about loneliness. In a time with more population and
connectability than ever before in human history, never has the
individual felt more faceless and disposable. We are slowly
sacrificing individuality and what it means to be human for the sake
of efficiency and being polite. Life passes quickly and, before we
know it, we take our decades of solitude and unresolved internal
dialogue with us to the grave.

---

### A Note on *Everyone Dies, but You Are Not Everyone*
My mother would often mention how, as a child, when someone
would speak about death, she would imagine herself running,
fleeing from death—forcing herself to stay alive by sheer will. The
concept of defying the inevitable has always fascinated me and
inspires a lot of my work. In many aspects this poem was also
inspired by Dylan Thomas’s famous poem Do not go gentle into that
good night, one of my favorite poems.

---

### A Note on *Lines*

This is a piece on the magnificent—and sometimes frightening—
beauty of aging. Time shapes us, and love must adapt. One day, we
may find ourselves embracing an entirely different person from the
one we fell in love with. This is not tragic, but beautiful. It is what
lifts love above transient emotion into something worthy of making
sense of our very existence.

---

### A Note on *Inorganic Matter*

*Inorganic Matter* is my best attempt at describing that painful, but
somehow strangely pleasant feeling of trying to remember what
someone you loved was like, but entirely failing each time.

There was a kind of fear that came over you when you met them,
but it's a euphoric, captivating fear. It's the kind that makes you
almost dread the prospect of being in their presence, but when you
are, you cherish every moment, and the hands on the clock circle
faster than you ever imagined possible.

There is a kind of shame that comes over you when you think of
them, but it's a sweet shame. It's the kind that makes you lean over
a puddle and stare at your reflection until a smile appears on your
face.

As years go by since you last met them and saw their face, a
strange thing begins to happen inside your head. Memory is an
unsettlingly sensitive thing. Hours of thought chisels them into
someone perhaps more similar to how you would like to remember
them rather than how they really were. Your memory of them and
who they were gradually deviates from reality and morphs into
someone new—or perhaps, something new.

In your mind their face, their voice, and their very personality
fossilize into a new, imaginary person that only exists inside your
own mind. This person is in no way more or less real than the
person you once knew and loved. It is only another version of them,
the one time would've shaped them to be if they had really only
lived for all that time up there, inside your head.

This is the message of *Inorganic Matter*: that existence is so much
more than observation through an organic body. It is the ultimate
declaration of love.
