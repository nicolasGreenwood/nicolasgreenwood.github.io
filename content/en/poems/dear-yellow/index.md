title: Dear Yellow,

about_this_poem: Yellow is a special color to me. Sometimes it strikes as the most gorgeous, joyful color I’ve seen, while other times I can’t bear to look at it. I haven’t quite make up my mind. <br><br>There is a specific yellow I particularly like, and that is this pale, pastille yellow I see very often here in Europe on historic, architectural buildings. For some reason, I see it everywhere I go.
---

“Dear yellow, you haunt me. You
bleed and shock
allure and soothe
pierce and cut
crack and fade.”

*You’re everywhere
I see you through the trees,
In sunlight, beneath my cheek;
You burn my eyes until
All I see is you
until I am yellow.*

“Dear yellow, why so pale? Do you
also age and weaken?
Will you also die one day?
These chips of you crumbling
between my fingers—are these your
cremated remains?”

*You’re everywhere
I see you passing by,
In the sky, on the walls;
You burn my eyes until
All I see is you
until I am yellow.*

“Dear yellow, I apologize. You are sweet,
and sour too; these hands, you see,
are also turning yellow, and these eyes,
when I turn them left and right
are yellow. So, you see
that I too am yellow,
and will age and weaken,
and you will one day
crumble my cremated remains
between your fingers.”

*You’re everywhere
I see you in the mirror,
In the fire, on my skin;
You burn my eyes until
All I see is you
until I am yellow.*
