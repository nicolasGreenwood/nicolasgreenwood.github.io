about_this_poem: Grass is uniquely poetic. Like stars, it simply exists, whether we notice it or not. This poem was partly inspired by Walt Whitman’s poem Song of Myself, 6 [A child said, What is the grass?], part of his collection Leaves of Grass, which is also widely considered one of the greatest pieces of American poetry ever written.
---

On blades of grass I lie;
they bend in the wind.

I breathe in and out,
my chest is hot and narrow;
the sky is cold and wide.

This scent of grass—
It fills my nostrils.

Grass can be torn and
grass can shred;
it can bleed a green paste
and wither in the sun.

But my cheek it tickles;
my shoulders it hugs
and caresses my neck.

It sways in the breeze, quiet;
it does not scream
nor laugh, nor weep:
it is grass.

Like dew, a cold tear
slides down; it is caught
by grass.

It does not hear.
It does not know.
It does not understand.
But tears—
it wipes away.

No time has passed.
The moment yet lives.

On blades of grass I lie;
they bend in the wind.
